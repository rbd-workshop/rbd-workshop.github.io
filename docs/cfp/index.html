Call for papers

The workshop invites submissions on capability retrieval, routing, and composition for language agents. Contributions may include research and position papers, benchmarks and datasets, reproducibility studies, demonstrations, and diagnostic or negative results.

We welcome work on retrieval augmented generation, memory, tool use, deep research, and systems with multiple agents when capability retrieval, routing, or composition is central to the contribution.

Topics of interest

  • Representing and indexing capabilities. Methods for describing and indexing tools, APIs, skills, workflows, memories, models, and agents using natural language, schemas, code, demonstrations, execution traces, graphs, ontologies, or embeddings. This includes indexes that change as capabilities are added or updated.
  • Retrieval, routing, and composition. Selecting individual capabilities or compatible combinations for a task. Topics include joint retrieval, dependencies between resources, and selection that accounts for permissions, reliability, and cost.
  • Retrieval during agent execution. Adapting retrieval to an agent’s state, plan, and observations. Topics include query reformulation, clarification, task decomposition, incorporating and invoking capabilities, delegation, replanning, recovery, and deciding when to abstain.
  • Evaluating capability use. Benchmarks and methods that distinguish errors in retrieval, incorporation, invocation, composition, and execution. Topics include whether a retrieved set contains all required capabilities, respects dependencies, and supports reliable task completion within resource constraints, as well as confidence calibration.
  • Capability registries and deployment. Dynamic and federated registries, access control, and Model Context Protocol ecosystems. Topics include provenance, permissions, keeping capability information current, monitoring system behavior, caching, reliability, and recovery.

Work that assumes a fixed, manually selected capability set is outside the workshop’s scope.

Submission guidelines

Draft author guidelines. The proposed paper limits, submission routes, and publication arrangements will be confirmed in the final call.

Paper preparation

  • Long papers: up to 8 pages of main content for substantial research contributions, systems, datasets, or evaluation studies.
  • Short papers: up to 4 pages of main content for focused contributions, position papers, work in progress, or carefully supported negative results.

References and appendices are excluded from these proposed limits. Prepare a single PDF using the official ACL style files in review mode. Remove author names and affiliations, and anonymize supplementary materials and linked resources.

Clearly describe the work’s limitations, evaluation methodology, and relevant ethical considerations. The main paper should contain the information needed to assess the contribution.

Submission and review

  • Direct submission: submit through the workshop’s OpenReview portal. Papers will receive double blind review, with three independent reviews planned for each submission.
  • ARR commitment: a separate route is planned for papers with completed ACL Rolling Review reviews. Eligible cycles and commitment instructions will be announced.

The submission portal and paper deadlines will be announced here.

Publication options

  • Archival: original, unpublished work intended for inclusion in the workshop proceedings, subject to the final publication arrangements.
  • Nonarchival: work intended for presentation and discussion without inclusion in the proceedings.

Both options are planned. The final call will explain how to select an option and the eligibility rules for previously published work, concurrent submissions, and cross-presentation.
