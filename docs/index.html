Retrieval Beyond Documents

Capability Retrieval for Language Agents

San Francisco, California · Workshop date to be announced

Overview

Retrieval Beyond Documents (RBD) brings together researchers in natural language processing, information retrieval, and agent systems to study capability retrieval for language agents.

Language agents increasingly need to retrieve tools, skills, workflows, memories, models, and other agents. These resources determine how a task is carried out. The central question is how to select a suitable capability, or a compatible set of capabilities, for an agent’s goal and current state.

The workshop examines how to represent and index capabilities, retrieve and compose them during execution, and evaluate their contribution to successful task completion. Contributions may include methods, benchmarks, systems, reproducibility studies, and diagnostic or negative results from academia and industry.

Call for papers

The workshop invites submissions on capability retrieval, routing, and composition for language agents. Contributions may include research and position papers, benchmarks and datasets, reproducibility studies, demonstrations, and diagnostic or negative results.

RAG, memory, tool use, deep research, and multiagent work is in scope when capability retrieval, routing, or composition is central.

Topics of interest

  • Representation & indexing. Represent tools, APIs, skills, workflows, memories, models, and agents through language, schemas, code, demonstrations, traces, graphs, and embeddings.
  • Retrieval, routing & composition. Retrieve and route across capability types, accounting for dependencies, compatibility, permissions, reliability, and cost. Compose the right set for the task.
  • Retrieval in the agent loop. Condition retrieval on execution state, plans, and observations. Study reformulation, clarification, decomposition, invocation, delegation, replanning, recovery, and abstention.
  • Action grounded evaluation. Evaluate retrieval, incorporation, invocation, composition, and execution. Develop complete set and dependency aware metrics, calibration, and resource constrained evaluation.
  • Ecosystems & deployment. Build dynamic, federated, and access controlled registries. Explore Model Context Protocol ecosystems, provenance, permissions, freshness, observability, caching, and reliability.

Submission guidelines

The submission portal and paper deadlines will be announced here.

The planned submission and review process is outlined below. Final arrangements will follow NAACL 2027 policy.

  • Paper format. Prepare an anonymized PDF using the official ACL style files. Page limits and instructions for supplementary material will be included in the final call.
  • Direct submissions. Papers submitted through OpenReview will receive double blind review. Each direct submission will receive three independent reviews.
  • ARR commitments. A commitment route for papers reviewed through ACL Rolling Review is planned. Eligible cycles and commitment instructions will be announced with the submission portal.
  • Publication options. Both archival and nonarchival submissions are planned. The final call will explain proceedings eligibility and how to select the appropriate option.
  • Research transparency. Authors are encouraged to describe limitations, relevant ethical considerations, and the data and evaluation procedures needed to understand their results. Code and supplementary resources should preserve anonymity during review.

Contributions should make capability retrieval, routing, or composition central. Work evaluating only a fixed, manually selected capability set falls outside the workshop’s scope.

Invited speakers

Talk titles and abstracts will be announced with the program.

Iryna Gurevych

TU Darmstadt

Iryna Gurevych is a Professor of Computer Science at TU Darmstadt and heads the Ubiquitous Knowledge Processing Lab. Her research connects semantic representations, information retrieval, and the reliability of language models. She co-authored Sentence-BERT, which makes semantic similarity search practical through reusable sentence embeddings, and BEIR, a benchmark for testing retrieval systems across diverse tasks and domains. Her broader work spans generative AI, AI safety, and AI for science, including the InterText project on relationships across documents. She is an ACL Fellow and a past ACL President, and received the Royal Society’s Milner Award and Lecture in 2025.

Selected work: Sentence-BERT · BEIR

Profile

Graham Neubig

Carnegie Mellon University

Graham Neubig is an Associate Professor at Carnegie Mellon University’s Language Technologies Institute, where he leads NeuLab, and Chief Scientist at OpenHands. His research spans language models, code generation, multilingual NLP, and agents that learn from experience. He co-authored OpenHands, an open platform for agents that write code, use the command line, and browse the web. His work on Agent Workflow Memory studies how agents can extract reusable workflows from past tasks and apply them to new problems. His later work on the OpenHands Software Agent SDK focuses on composable tools, memory, and sandboxed execution. He also develops open research software and freely available NLP teaching materials.

Selected work: OpenHands · Agent Workflow Memory · Software Agent SDK

Profile

Scott Wen-tau Yih

Meta Fundamental AI Research

Scott Wen-tau Yih is a Research Scientist at Meta Fundamental AI Research, working on neural retrieval and language models grounded in external knowledge. He co-authored Dense Passage Retrieval, which learns representations of questions and passages for open domain question answering, and the original RAG paper, which combines neural retrieval with text generation. His recent work on Reasoning Memory retrieves reusable procedural knowledge from earlier reasoning trajectories to guide new problems in mathematics, science, and coding. Previously, he held research positions at the Allen Institute for AI and Microsoft Research. He is an ACL Fellow and served as a program co-chair of EMNLP 2021.

Selected work: DPR · RAG · Reasoning Memory

Profile

Jimmy Lin

University of Waterloo

Jimmy Lin is a Professor and holds the David R. Cheriton Chair in the School of Computer Science at the University of Waterloo. His research brings together information retrieval, natural language processing, and data management, with a strong emphasis on reproducible systems. His work includes Pyserini, a toolkit for sparse, dense, and hybrid retrieval, and RankZephyr, an open language model for reranking search results. More recently, he co-authored AgentIR, which uses an agent’s reasoning trace alongside its query to improve retrieval during deep research. He is an ACM Fellow, an ACL Fellow, and a member of the SIGIR Academy.

Selected work: Pyserini · RankZephyr · AgentIR
