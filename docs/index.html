About the workshop

Retrieval Beyond Documents (RBD) brings together researchers in natural language processing, information retrieval, and agent systems to study capability retrieval for language agents.

Language agents must select tools, skills, workflows, memories, models, and other agents that determine how a task is carried out. Given a goal and the agent’s current state, how do we retrieve the right resource—or compatible set of resources—to use next?

The workshop focuses on representing and indexing capabilities, retrieving and composing them during execution, and evaluating their contribution to successful task completion.

We welcome perspectives from both academia and industry, including methods, benchmarks, systems, reproducibility studies, and diagnostic or negative results.

Call for papers

We invite archival and non-archival submissions on capability retrieval, routing, and composition for language agents. Contributions may include research and position papers, benchmarks and datasets, reproducibility studies, demonstrations, and diagnostic or negative results.

RAG, memory, tool-use, deep-research, and multi-agent work is in scope when capability retrieval, routing, or composition is central.

Topics of interest

Representation & indexing

Represent tools, APIs, skills, workflows, memories, models, and agents through language, schemas, code, demonstrations, traces, graphs, and embeddings.

Retrieval, routing & composition

Retrieve and route across capability types, accounting for dependencies, compatibility, permissions, reliability, and cost. Compose the right set for the task.

Retrieval in the agent loop

Condition retrieval on execution state, plans, and observations. Study reformulation, clarification, decomposition, invocation, delegation, replanning, recovery, and abstention.

Action-grounded evaluation

Evaluate retrieval, incorporation, invocation, composition, and execution. Develop complete-set and dependency-aware metrics, calibration, and resource-constrained evaluation.

Ecosystems & deployment

Build dynamic, federated, and access-controlled registries. Explore Model Context Protocol ecosystems, provenance, permissions, freshness, observability, caching, and reliability.

Submission and review process

Direct submissions are planned to be reviewed double-blind through OpenReview. An ARR commitment route and archival or non-archival options are planned, subject to NAACL policy. Work assuming a fixed, manually selected capability set falls outside the workshop’s focus.
