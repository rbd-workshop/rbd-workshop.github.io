Retrieval Beyond Documents

Capability Retrieval for Language Agents

San Francisco, California · Workshop date to be announced

Overview

Retrieval Beyond Documents (RBD) brings together researchers in natural language processing, information retrieval, and agent systems to study capability retrieval for language agents.

Language agents increasingly need to retrieve tools, skills, workflows, memories, models, and other agents. These resources determine how a task is carried out. The central question is how to select a suitable capability, or a compatible set of capabilities, for an agent’s goal and current state.

The workshop examines how to represent and index capabilities, retrieve and compose them during execution, and evaluate their contribution to successful task completion. Contributions may include methods, benchmarks, systems, reproducibility studies, and diagnostic or negative results from academia and industry.

Call for papers

The workshop invites submissions on capability retrieval, routing, and composition for language agents. Contributions may include research and position papers, benchmarks and datasets, reproducibility studies, demonstrations, and diagnostic or negative results.

RAG, memory, tool use, deep research, and multiagent work is in scope when capability retrieval, routing, or composition is central.

Topics of interest

  • Representation & indexing. Represent tools, APIs, skills, workflows, memories, models, and agents through language, schemas, code, demonstrations, traces, graphs, and embeddings.
  • Retrieval, routing & composition. Retrieve and route across capability types, accounting for dependencies, compatibility, permissions, reliability, and cost. Compose the right set for the task.
  • Retrieval in the agent loop. Condition retrieval on execution state, plans, and observations. Study reformulation, clarification, decomposition, invocation, delegation, replanning, recovery, and abstention.
  • Action grounded evaluation. Evaluate retrieval, incorporation, invocation, composition, and execution. Develop complete set and dependency aware metrics, calibration, and resource constrained evaluation.
  • Ecosystems & deployment. Build dynamic, federated, and access controlled registries. Explore Model Context Protocol ecosystems, provenance, permissions, freshness, observability, caching, and reliability.

Contributions should make capability retrieval, routing, or composition central. Work evaluating only a fixed, manually selected capability set falls outside the workshop’s scope.

Submission guidelines

Submission guidelines

Draft author guidelines. The proposed paper limits, submission routes, and publication arrangements will be confirmed in the final call.

Paper preparation

  • Long papers: up to 8 pages of main content for substantial research contributions, systems, datasets, or evaluation studies.
  • Short papers: up to 4 pages of main content for focused contributions, position papers, work in progress, or carefully supported negative results.

References and appendices are excluded from these proposed limits. Prepare a single PDF using the official ACL style files in review mode. Remove author names and affiliations, and anonymize supplementary materials and linked resources.

Clearly describe the work’s limitations, evaluation methodology, and relevant ethical considerations. The main paper should contain the information needed to assess the contribution.

Submission and review

  • Direct submission: submit through the workshop’s OpenReview portal. Papers will receive double blind review, with three independent reviews planned for each submission.
  • ARR commitment: a separate route is planned for papers with completed ACL Rolling Review reviews. Eligible cycles and commitment instructions will be announced.

The submission portal and paper deadlines will be announced here.

Publication options

  • Archival: original, unpublished work intended for inclusion in the workshop proceedings, subject to the final publication arrangements.
  • Nonarchival: work intended for presentation and discussion without inclusion in the proceedings.

Both options are planned. The final call will explain how to select an option and the eligibility rules for previously published work, concurrent submissions, and cross-presentation.

Iryna Gurevych is a Professor of Computer Science at TU Darmstadt and heads the Ubiquitous Knowledge Processing Lab. Her research spans natural language processing, semantic representations, information retrieval, and trustworthy AI. She studies how language technologies can work reliably with knowledge and support scientific research, with broader interests in generative AI, evaluation, and AI safety.
