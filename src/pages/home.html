Overview

Retrieval in NLP has traditionally focused on documents or passages that provide evidence. Language agents must also select tools, skills, workflows, memories, models, and other agents that determine how a task is carried out. Retrieval Beyond Documents (RBD) studies this problem, which we call capability retrieval: given a natural language goal and the agent’s current state, selecting the resource, or compatible set, to use next.

RBD centers four questions: how capabilities should be represented and indexed; retrieved or routed from language and execution state; selected and composed; and evaluated through downstream use. Capability relevance depends not only on semantic match, but also on whether a resource can perform the needed function, work with other resources, and be used in the current state. Interfaces, dependencies, permissions, cost, reliability, and execution history may therefore affect selection.

For NLP, this raises questions of grounding language to executable behavior, representing capabilities through descriptions, schemas, code, demonstrations, or traces, and updating retrieval as execution evolves. A document supplies evidence; a capability can change the agent’s action space.
